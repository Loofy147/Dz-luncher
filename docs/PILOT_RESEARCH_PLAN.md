# Pilot Research Plan

**Version:** 0.1  
**Market:** Algeria  
**Language:** Arabic-first; use register and Darja only where appropriate for the target audience.

## 1. Decisions this pilot must inform

1. Which initial business segment has a real, recent validation problem and willingness to pay?
2. Which report outputs would change a launch, pricing, distribution, or product decision?
3. Can an eligible respondent sample be recruited within a known time/cost range for specific target groups and wilayas?
4. Which transparent reward methods can be fulfilled reliably, lawfully, and affordably?
5. Can response-quality review protect clients without unfairly withholding legitimate participant rewards?
6. What collection, retention, hosting and transfer model is acceptable after legal review?

## 2. Discovery stage

### Business-side sample

Interview 10–15 founders or operators across at least three segments (for example, local services, retail/commerce, and digital products). These are purposive interviews, not a representative market survey.

For each interview, record:
- The last specific business decision they needed evidence for.
- Current alternatives: informal conversations, social posts, spreadsheets, agency research, or no research.
- Time and money spent, and what failure or delay cost them.
- Target population and geography.
- What decision rule or result would change their plan.
- Whether they would pay for a concrete sample size/report package at more than one price point.
- Evidence status: USER_REPORTED unless independently verified.

### Participant-side sample

Conduct 10–15 short interviews or usability tests with people who reflect intended pilot recruitment channels and varied mobile access. Do not treat this as a representative estimate of Algerian respondents.

Test understanding of:
- Study topic, estimated duration, eligibility and privacy notice.
- Reward value, conditions, timing, and what happens after a screen-out or manual review.
- How to report a problem or appeal a decision.
- Arabic question wording, option interpretation, and mobile form completion.

## 3. Concierge campaign pilot

Start with 2–3 low-risk studies: a local-service concept, a digital prototype/usability task, or a non-sensitive consumer concept. Avoid high-liability product sampling until safety and fulfilment requirements are reviewed.

Each campaign must have:
- One decision contract and a small set of falsifiable hypotheses.
- A defined audience, geography, screening criteria, recruitment sources, and quota plan.
- Reviewed Arabic wording, estimated duration, reward terms, privacy notice, and support route.
- A named human reviewer for response holds and disputes.
- A frozen instrument version and report specification.

## 4. Measurements

| Measure | Definition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Business activation | Businesses that provide a real decision brief and approve a study plan | Avoid counting casual interest as demand. |
| Paid intent | Deposits, signed pilot, or actual paid campaign; distinguish each level | Stated willingness to pay is not actual payment. |
| Recruitment time | Time from campaign opening to each accepted quota cell reaching target | Defines feasibility and avoids promises based on overall averages. |
| Cost per accepted completion | Acquisition and reward costs divided by accepted completions, reported by source/segment | Controls campaign economics. |
| Completion funnel | Views, starts, screen-outs, invalids, holds, accepted, quota-full | Shows recruitment and eligibility loss. |
| Quality review precision proxy | Share of human-reviewed holds judged unusable under a prewritten rubric | Requires human labels; is not universal fraud accuracy. |
| False-positive reversal rate | Share of holds later accepted after review/appeal | Directly measures harm to legitimate participants. |
| Reward fulfilment time | Time from reward obligation approval to successful fulfilment | Validates operational trust. |
| Report actionability | The next decision taken and why; follow up after 2–4 weeks | Tests business value, not just satisfaction. |

Do not tune response-quality thresholds against synthetic data alone. Freeze the policy during the first labelled pilot batch; record reviewer reasons and disagreement before considering changes.

## 5. Sample and reporting safeguards

- Online recruitment is not automatically representative of Algeria or every wilaya.
- Report counts by recruitment source, geography, relevant profile segment, screen-outs, holds, accepted responses, and quota gaps.
- Small segments remain inconclusive when below the predeclared minimum sample threshold.
- Wilson intervals describe sampling variability only; they do not correct self-selection or coverage bias.
- Declared purchase intention must not be presented as a completed purchase or validated demand.

## 6. Go/no-go gates

**Continue the pilot only if:** participants understand terms, rewards can be delivered, human review is available, privacy and legal gates are approved for the chosen flow, and reports can be reproduced from the frozen dataset.

**Do not scale yet if:** the business user cannot name a decision changed by the output, accepted completions are materially more expensive or slower than plausible willingness-to-pay supports, rewards cannot be reconciled, false-positive reversals are high, or data-transfer/processing requirements remain unresolved.

## 7. Recordkeeping

For every pilot, freeze and retain according to an approved retention policy: instrument version, recruitment source, field dates, quota configuration, policy version, aggregate sample flow, reviewer decisions/reasons, reward ledger reconciliation, report snapshot, and follow-up outcome. Do not retain raw identifying data longer than required for the approved purpose.
