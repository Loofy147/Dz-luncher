# ADR-0001: Two-sided business-validation marketplace

**Status:** Accepted for MVP discovery  
**Date:** 20 October 2026

## Context

Generic form creation does not solve the full business problem. Founders need help translating an idea into a decision, hypotheses, target audience, valid measures, and a defensible interpretation. Participants need transparent eligibility, time, privacy, rewards, and fair handling. The initial market is Algeria; the initial application language is Arabic RTL.

## Decision

Build a two-sided **business-validation and research marketplace** with shared survey/test execution and evidence reporting:

- Business side: decision contracts, study design, recruitment controls, quotas, reports, and team access.
- Participant side: discoverable studies, clear screening/reward terms, response status, and support/appeals.
- Trust/operations plane: quality signals, manual review, reward accounting/reconciliation, privacy records, and audit events.

Start with a controlled concierge pilot and a narrow survey engine. Do not build a generalized AI business adviser, unrestricted wallet, or high-liability product-testing marketplace as the first slice.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Generic form builder:** lower build cost but fails to differentiate on strategic validity, participant recruitment, reward operations, and evidence interpretation.
2. **Research agency only:** can provide high-touch quality but limits repeatable self-service and low-cost participation.
3. **Rewards/paid surveys only:** may recruit respondents but does not solve the founder's decision-design and report-quality problem.

## Consequences

- Product quality depends on sampling transparency and evidence labels, not merely the number of responses.
- Reward operations and privacy are first-class capabilities, not later add-ons.
- Early operations may be manual until real failure modes and unit economics are measured.
- Payment, hosting, legal entity, and provider choices remain OPEN pending evidence and Algerian legal review.

## Revisit triggers

Revisit this decision if discovery shows businesses pay only for a narrower service, respondent acquisition cannot be sustained for target segments, or reward/legal constraints make a two-sided marketplace nonviable.
